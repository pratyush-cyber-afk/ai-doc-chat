# Medical Log: Crew Sleep Disturbances

**Vessel:** CCV *Meridian Drift*
**Medical Officer:** Dr. Ana Reyes
**Period covered:** 16–22 March 2391
**Classification:** Survey: Medical in Confidence

---

## Background

From the night of 15–16 March, the day after the signal response event (IR-1649-02), crew members began reporting vivid, recurring dreams. By 18 March the reports were similar enough that I began logging them formally and collecting sleep monitor data.

The ship carries seven people. Six have reported the dream.

## Summary of the shared dream

Every affected crew member describes the same sequence, usually without having discussed it with anyone:

1. Descending through ice, slowly, with blue light above.
2. Entering a large round space. Several describe it as "a room too big to be inside the moon."
3. A sound like **many voices holding one note**.
4. A strong sense of being **welcomed** or **expected**.
5. Waking at the moment they try to speak.

Dr. Okafor pointed out that item 3 matches her private description of the signal's dense layer ("the Choir"). She has not shared that description with the rest of the crew.

Observations:

- Onset order roughly follows **time spent near the comms and science stations**.
- REM increase is well outside normal variation for confined-crew missions.
- Wake-up times cluster around **47-minute intervals** after sleep onset. This may be coincidence. I do not think it is.

## Clinical notes

**Lindqvist** is the most affected. He has stopped eating regularly and says the dream "keeps getting closer to the bottom." He has asked twice whether we are going to land.

**Okafor** is functioning well but has begun waking to take notes, which she says are "better than anything I write awake."

**Park** reports the mildest symptoms. She has asked for the away team to be screened before any landing is considered.

## Recommendations

1. Treat the shared dream as a **possible effect of the source**, not as stress, until shown otherwise.
2. **No crewed landing** at AS-14 until the effect is understood.
3. Rotate crew away from the science and comms stations where possible.
4. Report to Survey HQ with a request for specialist medical support.

## Note added 22 March

Recommendation 4 sent via Lethe-9 on 19 March. No reply.

Liaison Vasquez has advised that, under Handbook Section 12(a), sleep disturbance is not a classified hazard and does not affect duty status. She has asked me to describe the condition in reports as **"mission fatigue."**

I have not done so in this log.

*A. Reyes, Medical Officer*
